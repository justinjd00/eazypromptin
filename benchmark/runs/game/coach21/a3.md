The thing nobody tells you: **Claude Code can write all the code, but it can't draw a consistent set of pixel sprites.** If the art isn't decided now, you get mismatched placeholders forever. Usual fix: coloured shapes until the game is fun, then one consistent art source.

Assuming: 1 dungeon area, ~6-8 room layouts, 4 monster types + 1 boss, ~15 upgrades, 2 heroes in v1 · cute bouncy SFX from a free pack, music later · HUD = hearts, coins, current upgrades, partner's health · menus: main, host/join, pause, game over. Say if any of this feels too small or too big.

1. **Where does the pixel art come from?**
   a) Coloured shapes first, then one ready-made pixel pack once the game is fun (recommended: e.g. the free "0x72 Dungeon Tileset II" on itch.io is cute and fits exactly, ~16x16)
   b) I want to draw it myself (fine, but plan time: 2 heroes + 5 monsters + tiles is weeks of work)
   c) A friend or artist makes it
   d) AI-generated with a fixed style guide (cheap, but hard to keep consistent and animate)
2. **Setting, the cute flavour:**
   a) Classic fantasy dungeon with goofy slimes, bats and skeletons (recommended: easiest to find matching art)
   b) Candy/food dungeon: gummy monsters, cake rooms, a giant donut boss
   c) A kid's toy box or bedroom at night: plushies, toy soldiers, a jack-in-the-box boss
   d) Other, because…
3. **How should hitting things feel?**
   a) Punchy and arcadey: screen shake, monsters flash and pop, little hit-pauses (recommended, very cheap and makes the biggest difference)
   b) Calm and clean: minimal shake and flashes, easier on the eyes
4. **Heroes:** (decides how much art and balancing v1 needs)
   a) 2 heroes that only look different, same moves (recommended for v1)
   b) 2 heroes with different starting weapons, e.g. pistol vs. shotgun
   c) 1 hero in two colours, more later
