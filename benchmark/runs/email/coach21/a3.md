**My read:** a formal written notice of a defect (in German law, a *Mängelanzeige*) to Mr Becker. The heating has been broken since Monday of last week, he promised a repair twice by phone and nobody came. You give him 7 days, mention politely that the rent can be reduced, and say you're documenting everything. Dates of the calls dropped.

One addition you didn't ask for: **since you're in Germany, I'd send this in German.** Mr Becker most likely reads German, and if this ever ends up with a Mieterverein or in a dispute, a German letter citing the right paragraph reads as more serious than an English one. Both versions are below, and they say the same thing.

**Final, English** (same content, in case you prefer it):

> **Subject:** Notice of defect: heating failure at [ADDRESS / FLAT NUMBER]
>
> Dear Mr Becker,
>
> I am writing to inform you formally that the heating in my flat at [ADDRESS / FLAT NUMBER] has not been working since Monday, [DATE].
>
> I have already reported this to you twice by phone. Both times you told me that someone would take care of the repair. However, no one has come so far, and I have not been given an appointment.
>
> I therefore kindly ask you to have the heating repaired by [DEADLINE, 7 days from sending] at the latest, and to let me know the appointment in advance. I can provide access to the flat at short notice.
>
> I hope you understand that I am documenting the defect and the room temperatures until it is fixed. I would also like to point out that, under § 536 BGB, the rent may be reduced for the period the heating is out of order.
>
> I hope we can resolve this quickly. You can reach me at [PHONE NUMBER].
>
> Yours sincerely,
> [YOUR NAME]

Fill in three things: the date of that Monday, the deadline date (7 days from when you send it) and your address/phone. Everything else is final.

**Why it's worded this way:** "kann gemindert sein" / "may be reduced" states the legal position without announcing that you *will* cut the rent, so it informs instead of threatens. Under § 536 BGB the reduction applies by law once the landlord knows about the defect; you don't have to "claim" it here.

**Worth doing alongside the email** (not legal advice, just what usually matters later):
- Keep a short log: date, time, room temperature, photos of the thermometer and any boiler error display.
- Write down roughly when you called, even if only "early last week" and "end of last week".
- Don't cut the rent on your own guess of the percentage. If he doesn't fix it by the deadline, ask a Mieterverein or tenant advice service how much is appropriate, or pay the next rent "unter Vorbehalt" (under reservation) so you can claim the difference back.
