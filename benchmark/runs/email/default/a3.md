Got it. Here's a friendlier version that still puts everything on record. Since you're in Germany, I've written it in German, because your landlord will most likely expect that and it's the version that counts if it ever comes to a dispute. The English translation is below so you can check it.

---

**Betreff:** Heizungsausfall in der Wohnung [Adresse / Wohnungsnummer]

Hallo Herr/Frau [Name],

ich wollte mich noch einmal schriftlich wegen der Heizung melden. Sie ist seit dem [Datum] komplett ausgefallen, also inzwischen seit 9 Tagen. Wir hatten am [Datum] und am [Datum] telefoniert, und Sie hatten mir gesagt, dass jemand vorbeikommt. Bisher war leider noch niemand da.

Ich weiß, dass so etwas nicht immer sofort klappt, und ich bin in den drei Jahren hier immer gut mit Ihnen ausgekommen. Inzwischen wird es in der Wohnung aber wirklich kalt (zurzeit etwa [X] °C), deshalb wäre ich Ihnen dankbar, wenn die Heizung bis zum [Datum, 7 Tage ab heute] repariert werden könnte. Für einen Termin bin ich [Tage/Uhrzeiten] erreichbar, der Techniker kann sich auch gern direkt bei mir melden: [Telefonnummer].

Der Vollständigkeit halber möchte ich noch erwähnen, dass ich den Ausfall und die Temperaturen dokumentiere und mir vorbehalte, die Miete für den Zeitraum ohne Heizung angemessen zu mindern.

Vielen Dank schon mal und viele Grüße
[Ihr Name]

---

**English translation:**

Subject: Heating failure in the apartment [address / unit]

Hello Mr./Ms. [Name],

I wanted to follow up in writing about the heating. It has been completely out since [date], so 9 days now. We spoke on [date] and [date], and you told me someone would come by. Unfortunately, nobody has come yet.

I know these things don't always happen right away, and I've always gotten along well with you in my three years here. But the apartment is getting really cold now (currently around [X] °C), so I'd be grateful if the heating could be repaired by [date, 7 days from today]. I'm available for an appointment [days/times], and the technician is welcome to contact me directly at [phone number].

For completeness, I'd like to mention that I'm documenting the outage and the temperatures, and I reserve the right to reduce the rent appropriately for the period without heating.

Thanks in advance and best regards,
[Your name]

---

**Good to know in Germany:**

- **Rent reduction (Mietminderung, § 536 BGB) applies automatically by law** once you've reported the defect, which you've now done in writing. You don't need the landlord's approval. That's why the email can stay friendly: you're just stating a right you already have, not threatening anything.
- **Don't just pay less rent next month.** How much you can reduce depends on the case (total failure during the heating season can justify a large reduction, but courts vary). If you get the amount wrong, it can cause real trouble. The safer route is to pay the full rent **"unter Vorbehalt"** (under reservation) and settle the reduction afterwards. Put that phrase in the payment reference or tell the landlord in writing.
- **Documentation:** write down the date and temperature each day (a photo of a thermometer works well), and keep this email plus notes on both phone calls.
- **Space heaters:** keep the receipt, and note that the extra electricity cost may be recoverable too.
- **If nothing happens after 7 days,** your local Mieterverein (tenants' association, usually around 60 to 100 € a year including legal advice) is the best next step before doing anything else.
